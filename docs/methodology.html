Methodology and sources

How the map is built, what it leaves out and where each number comes from.

This map combines three official, regulator-maintained license rolls into a single view. Every dot is a current license held by a business at a specific address in the five boroughs. Nothing on the map comes from third-party directories, review sites or tips.

What is included

CategorySourceFilterGeocoding
Liquor stores New York State Liquor Authority — Current Liquor Authority Active Licenses on the Open Data NY portal. description IN ('Liquor Store', 'Wine Store') and the premises county is one of the five boroughs. These are off-premises (package store) licenses only; bars and restaurants are not included, and grocery beer licenses are a separate layer. Under New York law, liquor stores cannot sell beer. Latitude and longitude come from the dataset's built-in georeference field. Where that field puts two different addresses on the same point (it often collapses unhyphenated Queens addresses such as "98 02 Jamaica Ave" onto one spot per street), the address is geocoded again with GeoSearch and the new point is kept only if it passes the check described under legal cannabis.
Beer (groceries, delis, drug stores) New York State Liquor Authority — same dataset as liquor stores. description IN ('Grocery Store', 'Drug Store') and the premises county is one of the five boroughs. These are off-premises beer licenses, the universe of bodegas, delis, supermarkets and drug stores allowed to sell beer, cider and wine coolers. New York grocery stores cannot sell wine or liquor. This category is hidden by default on the map because it is the largest single layer. Same as liquor stores.
Legal cannabis dispensaries New York State Office of Cannabis Management — Current OCM Licenses. Active adult-use retail dispensary licenses, conditional adult-use retail dispensary licenses, registered organization dispensaries and adult-use microbusinesses with retail activity authorized. License status must be Active and the county must be one of the five boroughs.

An active license does not mean a store is open. The office's operational_status field separates the two: dispensaries marked Active there are drawn as solid dots and counted as open; the rest, which hold a license but are not serving customers, are drawn as rings. In November 2026 about a quarter of the city's licensed dispensaries were in that second group.		 OCM does not publish coordinates. Addresses are geocoded with the New York City Department of City Planning's GeoSearch API. GeoSearch knows only New York City and always returns its closest guess, so a result is kept only when its house number and ZIP code match the license record. A handful of records fail that check each week; they are kept in the downloadable CSV but left off the map rather than drawn in the wrong place.
Tobacco and e-cigarette retailers New York City Department of Consumer and Worker Protection — Issued Licenses (formerly Legally Operating Businesses). business_category IN ('Tobacco Retail Dealer', 'Electronic Cigarette Dealer') and license status is Active or Ready for Renewal. Licenses held by the same business at the same address are merged: a storefront that holds both a tobacco and an e-cigarette license appears as one dot labeled "Tobacco + e-cigarette." Latitude and longitude come from the dataset's built-in coordinate fields.

Refresh schedule

The data is rebuilt automatically once a week from the original sources. The footer of the map shows the date of the most recent refresh. The full build script is in this project's GitHub repository.

What is not included

  • Bars and restaurants. On-premises liquor licenses (restaurants, additional bars, clubs, hotels) are excluded. The map covers retail takeaway only.
  • Unlicensed cannabis or smoke shops. The OCM dataset contains only legal, licensed dispensaries. Unlicensed sellers — the bulk of New York City's smoke shops since 2022 — are by definition not in any regulator's database. The New York City Sheriff's Office tracks shops shut down under Operation Padlock to Protect, but those records are released as press notices rather than as a clean dataset, so they are not currently incorporated here.
  • Gambling, adult entertainment and other vice categories. Out of scope for this version.
  • Records outside the city. A few records on the state rolls carry a city county but a Long Island, Hudson Valley or upstate address. A record stays on the map only if its ZIP code lies wholly inside the city or its point falls inside the borough boundaries published by the Department of City Planning. The records dropped each week are listed in methodology.json.

Limitations

  • A current license is not a guarantee that the business is open. Licensees can close, move or transfer ownership without immediately notifying the regulator.
  • Co-located businesses. A single storefront can hold multiple licenses (for example, a bodega may be both a tobacco dealer and a beer-licensed grocery). On the map these appear as overlapping dots in different categories.
  • Geocoding errors. Most addresses geocode cleanly, but a small number resolve to the centroid of a long block or an incorrect side of a street, or fail outright. The downloadable CSV preserves the original address as reported by the regulator.
  • Cannabis license status churn. The OCM dataset changes weekly as new licenses are issued, surrendered or moved between conditional and full status. The map shows whatever status was current at the time of the last refresh.
  • Tobacco license deduplication. More than a third of New York City's tobacco-licensed storefronts also hold a separate Electronic Cigarette Dealer license. Without merging, those addresses would appear as two stacked dots and count twice in the legend. The build script merges licenses held by the same business at the same building number, street, unit and ZIP into a single feature labeled "Tobacco + e-cigarette." The license-count field on each feature records how many licenses were merged. Different businesses sharing a building, such as shops in a mall or a transit terminal, stay separate.
  • Liquor and beer at the same address. New York's liquor law forbids holding an off-premises liquor license and an off-premises beer license at the same physical premises. About two dozen addresses in the data nonetheless appear in both categories, a small fraction of 1%. These are almost always multi-tenant buildings (corner storefronts, malls, mixed-use buildings) where two distinct businesses share an address string. They are left as separate dots.

Reproducing this

The full build pipeline is a single Python script with no dependencies beyond the standard library. It pulls each source via Socrata's open API, filters and normalizes the records, geocodes addresses that lack reliable coordinates against GeoSearch with a local cache, drops records outside the city and writes a merged GeoJSON, a CSV and a metadata file. If any category comes back more than 30% smaller than the week before, the build stops and the previous week's data stays up, since a drop that size is far more likely to be a broken download than a real change. See scripts/build_data.py in the GitHub repository.
